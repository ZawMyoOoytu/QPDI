\# QPDI — Quantum-Assisted Political Decision Intelligence



\*\*QPDI (Quantum-Assisted Political Decision Intelligence)\*\* is a research-oriented decision-support framework that explores how \*\*Artificial Intelligence (AI), mathematical optimization, and Quantum Computing\*\* can be combined for synthetic public-policy resource allocation problems.



> \*\*Research Disclaimer:\*\* QPDI is a synthetic research and educational framework. It is \*\*not\*\* a system for real-world political prediction, voter manipulation, electoral targeting, or automated political decision-making.



\---



\## 1. Overview



Public-policy planning often involves selecting a limited number of regions, projects, or interventions from a larger candidate set while balancing multiple objectives such as:



\* estimated policy benefit

\* population needs

\* resource constraints

\* optimization quality

\* computational cost



QPDI investigates whether a hybrid \*\*AI + Quantum Optimization\*\* pipeline can provide a useful approach to these combinatorial decision problems.



The current research pipeline is:



```text

&#x20;       ↓

&#x20;       ↓

&#x20;       ↓

&#x20;       ↓

```

\## 2. Research Objective



The main objective of QPDI is to investigate the following question:



> \*\*Can quantum optimization techniques, when combined with AI-based candidate selection, effectively solve constrained public-policy optimization problems?\*\*



The project focuses on methodological evaluation rather than claiming quantum advantage.



In particular, QPDI evaluates:



1\. AI-assisted candidate selection

2\. QUBO formulation

3\. QUBO → Ising Hamiltonian mapping

4\. QAOA optimization

5\. Quantum measurement distributions

6\. Classical exact optimization

7\. Quantum-vs-classical solution quality

8\. Parameter sensitivity

9\. Reproducibility and validation

\## 4. Mathematical Formulation



QPDI represents candidate selection using binary variables:



```text

xᵢ ∈ {0,1}

```



where:



\* `xᵢ = 1` means candidate `i` is selected

\* `xᵢ = 0` means candidate `i` is not selected



The optimization problem is represented as a QUBO:



```text

E(x) = xᵀ Q x

```



The objective is to find:



```text

x\* = argmin E(x)

```



subject to the required selection constraints.



For the quantum formulation, binary variables are mapped to Pauli-Z operators using:



```text

xᵢ = (1 - Zᵢ) / 2

```



The resulting Ising Hamiltonian can then be used as the cost Hamiltonian for QAOA.



\---



\## 5. Quantum Optimization with QAOA



QPDI uses the \*\*Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)\*\* to search for low-energy solutions.



Conceptually:



```text

|+⟩ state

&#x20;  ↓

Cost Hamiltonian

&#x20;  ↓

Mixer Hamiltonian

&#x20;  ↓

QAOA Layers

&#x20;  ↓

Measurement

&#x20;  ↓

Candidate Bitstrings

```



The QAOA parameters include:



\* `γ` (gamma)

\* `β` (beta)



These parameters determine the evolution generated by the cost and mixer Hamiltonians.

\## 7. QAOA Parameter Sensitivity



QPDI includes a parameter-scan experiment to investigate the sensitivity of QAOA performance to `γ` and `β`.



The current scan evaluated:



```text

Grid points : 41

Evaluations : 1681

```



The best parameter point found in the scan was:



```text

Gamma             : 1.992229488

Beta              : 1.884955592

Expected energy   : -76.246657494

```



Compared with the classical optimum:



```text

Classical optimum : -79.838229312

Energy gap         : 3.591571817

Relative gap       : 4.498561%

```



The best point was not located at a parameter boundary:



```text

Gamma near 0      : False

Gamma near 2π     : False

Beta at boundary  : False

Boundary warning  : False

```



This suggests that the observed QAOA performance is not simply caused by selecting an obvious parameter-boundary value.

\## 9. Important Research Limitation



\### No Quantum Advantage Has Been Demonstrated



The current QPDI results \*\*do not demonstrate quantum advantage\*\*.



The classical exact solution remains better than the current QAOA expected-energy result:



```text

QAOA expected energy : -76.246657494

Classical optimum    : -79.838229312

Relative gap         : 4.498561%

```



Therefore, the appropriate research conclusion is:



> \*\*The current implementation demonstrates a functioning quantum-assisted optimization workflow, but it does not establish quantum advantage over classical optimization.\*\*



This distinction is important because successful execution of a quantum algorithm is not equivalent to demonstrating computational advantage.

\## 15. Research Philosophy



QPDI follows several principles:



\### 1. Quantum ≠ Automatically Better



Using a quantum algorithm does not automatically produce a better solution.



\### 2. Classical Benchmarking Is Essential



Quantum results should be compared against appropriate classical baselines.



\### 3. Small Demonstrations Are Not Proof of Scalability



A small simulation can validate methodology, but it cannot establish large-scale computational advantage.



\### 4. Synthetic Data Reduces Real-World Risk



The current project uses synthetic policy data rather than attempting to model or manipulate real populations or political outcomes.



\### 5. Human Oversight



Potential real-world decision-support systems should preserve human accountability, governance, transparency, and auditability.
